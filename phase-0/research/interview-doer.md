# Doer interview — 40 minutes

**Goal:** find out what they earn now, what they will actually accept, and what would make them
leave. Supply churn is what kills marketplaces quietly; a doer who signs up and does nothing is
worse than one who never signed up, because they distort every number.

Same rule: past behaviour, not stated intent. Record with consent, delete after synthesis.

---

## 0. Setup (2 min)

"I'm looking at a platform where people post small tasks — phone calls, research, paperwork — and
others do them for money. I want to understand how you earn now and what would make this worth
your time. I'm not hiring today, though I may have real paid work this month."

## 1. Their time and money now (12 min)

1. Walk me through a typical weekday. Where are the gaps?
2. What have you earned money from in the last six months? How much, roughly?
3. How did you find that work?
4. What was the worst part of it? _(Listen for: payment delays, commute, disrespect, uncertainty.)_
5. Have you used a gig or freelance platform? Which? What happened?
6. If you stopped using it — why exactly? _(This is the single most useful answer in the script.)_

## 2. Rate and acceptance (10 min)

7. What is the least you would accept for an hour of work from your phone, at home?
8. Would you do a 20-minute task for ₹100? What about a 40-minute insurance call for ₹150?
9. How long would you wait for payment after finishing? What is too long?
10. If the platform took 18% of that ₹150 — you receive ₹123 — is that still worth doing?
    _(Ask exactly this way. Record objections word for word: this is the test for
    [A6](../assumptions.md).)_
11. What would make you say no to a task that pays fine?

## 3. Capability and category (8 min)

12. What could you do better than most people? Anything from a previous job, a degree, a hobby?
13. Are you comfortable calling a stranger — an insurance company, a landlord, a government
    office — on someone else's behalf?
14. If a requester gave you their policy number and claim details, how would you feel about
    holding that?
15. Would you go somewhere in person — a government office, a bank — for ₹300 plus travel?
    _(Supply-side test for [ADR-0007](../../docs/decisions/ADR-0007-physical-errands.md).)_
16. Do you have PAN, a bank account in your own name, and a smartphone that runs current apps?

## 4. Reaction and the close (8 min)

Describe it in one sentence: *"You'd see a feed of tasks in your categories, apply, do the work
through the app, and get paid within 24 hours of the requester approving it."*

17. What's your first reaction?
18. What would make you check the app tomorrow morning?
19. What would make you stop checking it? _(Expect "nothing in the feed". Ask how many empty
    visits it would take.)_
20. **The real test:** I have some real paid tasks this month. Can I send you one this week?
    _(A yes here, followed by actually doing the task, is the only evidence that counts.)_

## Immediately afterwards (5 min)

- Their floor rate, in their words, and their reaction to the 18% cut.
- What they are unusually good at.
- Would they take an in-person task?
- Verdict: would this person still be active in month two? yes / no / unclear — and why.

File as `interviews/D-NN-firstname.md`, add a row to `participants.csv`, and if they said yes to
question 20, put them straight into the concierge roster.
