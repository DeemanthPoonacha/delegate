# Requester interview — 45 minutes

**Goal:** find out what people already do about tasks they do not want to do, and what they have
already paid to avoid. Not whether they like Delegate.

**Rule for the whole conversation:** ask about the past, not the future. "Would you use this?" is
worthless. "What did you do last time?" is evidence. Never describe the product before section 4,
and when you do, stop talking and watch their face.

Record with consent. Delete the recording after synthesis (DPDP-5).

---

## 0. Setup (2 min)

"I'm working on something around delegating everyday tasks. I'm not selling anything today — I
want to understand how you handle this stuff now. There are no wrong answers, and the boring
details are the useful part. Can I record this? I'll delete it once I've written my notes."

## 1. Their week (10 min)

1. Walk me through last week. Where did the time go that you did not choose?
2. What is on your list right now that has been there more than two weeks?
3. Why has it not been done? _(Listen for: no time, don't know how, dread, keeps getting bumped.)_
4. Who else in your household or team could do it? Why haven't they?
5. What happens if it never gets done?

## 2. What they already pay for (12 min)

6. Tell me about the last time you paid someone to do something you could technically have done
   yourself. _(Agent, CA, broker, cleaner, consultant, cousin with a laptop.)_
7. How did you find that person? What made you trust them?
8. What did you pay? How did the price get set?
9. What went wrong, if anything? What did you do about it?
10. Would you use them again? Why?

> This section carries the interview. Someone who has paid a ₹2,000 agent to stand in a queue has
> already validated the category with their wallet. Someone who has never paid for help is telling
> you about a preference, not a purchase.

## 3. The specific tasks (12 min)

For each of these, ask: **has this happened to you in the last six months?** If yes: what did you
do, how long did it take, and what was that time worth? If no, move on — do not ask them to
imagine it.

- Comparing hotels or flights for a trip
- An insurance claim or a policy renewal
- A government or municipal process — EPF, passport, licence, property
- Being on hold with a support line or fighting for a refund
- Comparing prices across sites before a purchase
- Filling a long form with details you already have somewhere
- Finding a flat, a school, a doctor, a vendor

Then: **of everything we just talked about, which one would you hand to a competent stranger
tomorrow?** And: **which one would you absolutely not?** The second answer is the more useful one.

## 4. Reaction (7 min)

Now describe it, in one sentence, and stop: *"A marketplace where you post a task — anything from
a 20-minute phone call to a six-month project — someone verified picks it up, and your money is
held until you approve the result."*

11. What's your first reaction?
12. What would have to be true for you to try it once?
13. What's the thing that would stop you?
14. Who do you imagine is on the other side? _(This tells you what trust signals the profile needs.)_

## 5. Price and the close (5 min)

15. Take the task you said you'd hand over. What would you expect that to cost?
16. At ₹199, would you have tried it last month? At ₹499?
17. **The real test:** I'm running a few of these by hand this month. Can I do that task for you
    this week, at [price]? _(If yes, book it on the spot and log it in the concierge run. This
    single question is worth more than the previous 44 minutes.)_
18. Who else should I talk to?

## Immediately afterwards (5 min, do not skip)

Write, while it is fresh:

- The one sentence they said that you did not expect.
- Tasks they named, in their words. Categorise later, not now.
- Did they agree to a real task? At what price?
- Verdict: would this person pay again? yes / no / unclear.

File it as `interviews/R-NN-firstname.md` and add a row to `participants.csv`.
